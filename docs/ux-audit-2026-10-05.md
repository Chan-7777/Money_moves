# MoneyMoves AU: UX + UI production-readiness audit

**Mode:** FIRST AUDIT · **Date:** 2026-10-05 · **Target:** https://moneymoves-au.vercel.app (live)
**Journey traced:** Landing → sign-in → 4-step form → result → paid tier → PayPal (stopped at the PayPal buttons; no payment made)
**Stage / users:** MVP · Australians aged 25–40 with consumer debt or HECS debt
**Stack:** Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, `design-system/tokens.css`, Vercel functions, Clerk (auth), Firebase RTDB, PayPal Orders API

**Evidence used**
- Live pixels at 1440×900 desktop and 390×844 mobile (Chrome DevTools)
- The live accessibility tree and the live console
- Source at commit `d042039`

**Limits**
- I didn't sign in with a real Google account. The sign-in overlay is client-side, so I hid it locally to reach the form.
- I didn't click "Download my free PDF" or "Send it", because each uses a real beta spot or sends a real email.
- I didn't complete a payment.
- Everything after the PayPal button is judged from code only and marked that way.

---

## A. Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Strongest UX decision** | The four-step form takes rough estimates and turns them into one named first move ("Build a 1-month emergency buffer (A$3,965) — you're A$2,465 short"), with a ranked debt table whose numbers I checked by hand (surplus 5,200 − 3,400 − 565 = 1,235). For this audience, that's the product. |
| **Biggest UX failure** | The price is different everywhere and the paid product isn't what's sold. The landing page and checkout say **A$49/month, "cancel anytime with 1 click", "ongoing monthly rate defense"**. The checkout makes a **one-off** PayPal capture (`intent=capture`, Orders API) with no subscription, no cancel flow and no monthly service in the codebase. The FAQ on the same page says **A$39 / A$59**. |
| **Biggest UI failure** | The production site runs Clerk **development keys**. The sign-in modal says **"Sign in to My Application"** with an orange **"Development mode"** label, and it stacks over the product's own sign-in card. It's the first screen every user sees. |
| **Most cognitive load** | The result page. It shows five competing blocks (plan, three KPIs, a "Diagnostic Alert" upsell, a debt table, three action cards, email, share, a beta PDF card holding a second paid pitch, a car re-run and a disclaimer) in one 2,800 px scroll. Three of them ask for money or an email. |
| **Where users lose confidence** | The orange "Diagnostic Alert: Unseen Financial Leak". Its "11 months earlier freedom" is `debts.length × 3.5`. Its dollar "leak" has a A$1,200 floor, and a debt-free user is shown a A$850 leak. Anyone who checks the maths will distrust everything else on the page. |
| **Where users waste the most time** | Wanting the car comparison after seeing results. The only path is "Re-run →", which repeats all four steps. Second: a header about 260 px tall (desktop) to 440 px (mobile) repeats above every step. |
| **What could be automated** | HECS rate and minimum (rate = indexation, minimum = 0, since it's repaid through tax). Pay-cycle conversion of minimums. The 1-month buffer target (already computed, so it could be shown as the default goal). The email address (Clerk already has it from sign-in). |
| **What to remove entirely** | The "Diagnostic Alert" block and its invented numbers. The "100x Guarantee" naming. The A$149 decoy tier (it exists only to anchor A$49). The "1 Australians used this" stat. |
| **The single biggest improvement** | One honest price that matches what the server charges, shown in one place (after results, not before), with the free plan kept visible next to it. |
| **What would make it exceptional** | "Show your working" on every number: each ranking and dollar figure expands to its formula and inputs. The AusFinance audience trusts maths it can audit, and none of the competition shows it. |

## Phase 0: Understanding the product

- **What it does:** Takes take-home pay, living costs, savings, debts, car intent and a priority. Returns a ranked "first move" plus a debt payoff order. Sells a PDF "dossier".
- **For whom:** Australians aged 25–40 with a credit card, car loan or HECS debt who won't pay A$4k for a planner.
- **Primary goal:** "Tell me what to do first with my money, and why."
- **Most important journey:** landing → form → result (the value) → paid PDF (the business).
- **What the user must understand:** their #1 move, how much it costs or saves, and that this is general information.
- **Essential:** first move, surplus, buffer, debt order. **Secondary:** reasoning ("Why this rank"), PDF. **Tertiary:** sharing, voting, stickers.
- **Ambiguities (not guessed):**
  1. What is the real business model, monthly or one-off? The code says one-off; the copy says monthly.
  2. What should a paid "Active Co-Pilot" deliver after day 1? Nothing in `api/` sends monthly check-ins or RBA alerts.
  3. Is the beta still giving free PDFs (the counter shows 50/50 remaining) while the paid tiers are also pushed?
  4. Is the email form's "one-line monthly check-in" actually sent? Can't be determined from the evidence available.

## Phase 1: UX principles

### 1. Zero confusion: **4/10**
- **Observation:** The landing page promises "Sign in with Google". You click and get the product card, then 3 s later a second modal saying "Sign in to My Application" with email, phone and Sign-up options.
- **Why it matters:** At the first gate the user can't tell whose site they're signing into.
- **Specific problem:**
  - CTA wording varies: "Try it free →", "Sign in with Google — get your free plan", "Claim beta free spot or start co-pilot →" (one button, two opposite outcomes, linking to `#signup`, which is just the bottom CTA).
  - "100x Guarantee" doesn't say 100x of what.
- **Fix:**
  - One CTA label everywhere: "Get my free plan".
  - Pricing card buttons: "Get my free plan" (free) / "Buy the full plan – A$X" (paid).
  - Rename the guarantee: "30-day money-back guarantee".
- **Priority:** Critical

### 2. Goal-oriented design: **6/10**
- **Observation:** Four steps, mostly about the user's situation. Good.
- **Problems:**
  - Step 3 is one yes/no question with its own Next.
  - HECS asks for a rate and a minimum it doesn't have.
  - The car comparison is only reachable by re-running.
  - The email is asked for again after Google sign-in.
- **Fixes:**
  - Merge Step 3 into Step 4 (the "Considering a car?" toggle under the priority chips), so the progress bar becomes 3 steps.
  - Default the HECS fields.
  - Prefill the email from Clerk.
  - Make the car comparison inline.
- **Priority:** High

### 3. Cognitive load: **4/10**
- **Observation:** The result page stacks 11 blocks. Three money asks (Diagnostic Alert → paid, beta PDF → paid co-pilot nested inside it, email capture) and one share block. The landing page repeats the price anchor (A$149 vs A$49) twice and the free/paid split three times.
- **Fix:**
  - One money ask per screen.
  - On results: one "Next step" card, as in the Phase 9 wireframe.
  - Move share and vote into the footer.
- **Priority:** High

### 4. Progressive disclosure: **7/10**
- **Observation:** The "▸ Why this rank" disclosure is the right pattern. The HECS card, though, dumps 90 words of policy (indexation, 20% cut, A$67k threshold) into the always-visible body.
- **Fix:** Card body: one sentence ("HECS grows only with inflation (2.8%). Pay your 20.99% card first."). Move the rest into "Why this rank".
- **Priority:** Medium

### 5. Feedback and system status: **5/10**
- **Observations:**
  - Inline validation is good ("Please enter your take-home pay.").
  - The sign-in click dims the screen for about 3 s with no spinner.
  - The landing spot counter renders "— of 50" until Firebase responds.
  - "⏳ Processing payment…" covers PayPal capture, DOCX generation, ConvertAPI and Resend (code). That's probably 5–20 s with no stage feedback (cannot confirm timing without a live payment).
- **Fix:**
  - A spinner inside the Google button while `Clerk.openSignIn()` resolves.
  - Hide the spot-counter line until data arrives (no em-dash placeholder).
  - Staged payment copy: "Payment confirmed ✓ → Building your PDF… → Emailing…".
- **Priority:** High

### 6. Error handling and recovery: **5/10**

| Error | What happened / why / what to do / retry / data lost? | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| Empty step-1 fields | Clear, inline. But focus stays on the Next button and the errors aren't announced (no `aria-describedby`, no `aria-invalid`). | Keep the copy. Focus the first invalid input and link the error by id. |
| Rate 20.99 | `step=0.1` marks it invalid; no message is shown, and the app accepts it anyway. A silent inconsistency. | Fix the step (see C8). |
| Sign-in failure | "Sign-in failed — please try again." No reason, no alternative. | "We couldn't reach Google sign-in. Check your connection and try again. Your answers are saved in this browser." |
| PayPal `onError` | "Payment error. Please refresh and try again." A refresh resets the result screen (the localStorage draft keeps only income/expenses/savings, not debts). | "PayPal couldn't start the payment. You haven't been charged. [Try again]". Re-render the buttons without reloading. |
| Capture failure | "Something went wrong capturing your payment: {err.message}". The user doesn't know whether they were charged. | "We couldn't confirm your payment. If PayPal shows a charge, email support@… with order {orderID} and we'll send your PDF within 24 h." Always show the order ID. |
| No email after payment | Red "We could not email your PDF (email delivery issue)". It's not an error. | "Your PDF is ready. Download it below." in muted text. |

**Priority:** High

### 7. Information hierarchy: **4/10**
- **Landing:**
  - *Primary:* the H1 and the Get-plan CTA.
  - *Secondary:* how it works.
  - *Tertiary:* the quote, the stats.
  - *Loudest element:* the nav's dark "Try it free →" and the 48 px "A$49" price. The hero CTA is a white outlined button and visually the weakest button on the page.
- **Result:**
  - *Primary:* "Build a 1-month emergency buffer".
  - *Secondary:* debt order.
  - *Tertiary:* share, vote.
  - *Loudest:* the red-bordered plan card (reads as an error) and the orange alert with A$2,519 in 20 px red. The upsell outshouts the answer.
- **Fix:** See Top-10 #9 and Phase 2.1.
- **Priority:** High

### 8. Speed and perceived performance: **6/10**
- **Observation:** Steps switch instantly (client-side). The landing CTA does a full page load to `app.html`, which then shows a blank dimmed overlay for about 3 s. The "Next" click scrolls smoothly over about 500 ms.
- **Fix:**
  - `<link rel="preconnect" href="https://<clerk-frontend-api>">` on `index.html`.
  - Load Clerk on the landing page and open sign-in there, so `app.html` opens already authenticated.
- **Priority:** Medium

### 9. Trust: **2/10**
- **Observation:** For a finance tool, this is the score that matters, and the product gets it wrong in the places users look hardest:
  1. Fabricated leak and "months earlier" figures (code-verified).
  2. A monthly subscription that doesn't exist.
  3. Three different price sets on one domain.
  4. A dev-mode auth modal.
  5. "1 Australians used this", which is negative social proof and ungrammatical.
  6. "Nothing is stored" / "Financial data never stored", while answers persist in `localStorage` (`moneymoves_draft`) and the paid flow sends the full state to ConvertAPI (a third party) to render the PDF.
  7. HECS shows "0%" in the table but "2.8%" in the card.
  8. "Institutional-grade" and "Strategy Dossier tailored to your exact debt balances" sit next to the "not personal advice" footer. That framing invites ASIC's general vs personal advice question. (A product risk to check with a compliance adviser; I'm not giving a legal opinion.)
- **Fix:**
  - Delete the invented figures.
  - One price.
  - Hide the user-count stat until it's at least 100.
  - Change the badge to "Your numbers stay in this browser. We don't keep them." and disclose ConvertAPI in `privacy.html`.
  - Show HECS as "2.8% (indexation)" in both places.
  - Add a visible "Last updated: FY2025-26 rates, May 2026" line beside the plan (it currently sits only in the disclaimer).
- **Priority:** Critical

### 10. Next best action: **5/10**
- **Observation:**
  - Result: the next step is "build buffer", but there's no concrete "how" (no "A$1,235 surplus → about 2 months to fill the buffer").
  - Car: a dead end (re-run).
  - After payment (code): a download button. No "what to do this week".
- **Fix:**
  - Under the plan headline, add: "At your A$1,235/mo surplus, that's **2 months**. Then the credit card is gone in **~4 months**." Computed, not invented.
- **Priority:** High

## Phase 2: UI principles

### 1. Visual hierarchy and focal point: **4/10**
- **Observation:**
  - **Landing at 1440:** the eye lands on the H1 (good), then jumps to the dark nav pill. The hero CTA is `#ffffff` with a 1 px `#e8e6e1` border, which is less weight than the plain text around it.
  - **Results:** the eye lands on the red A$2,519.
- **Fix:**
  - Hero CTA: solid `background: var(--color-accent) #4a7c59; color: #fff; height: 52px; padding: 0 24px; font: 700 16px; radius: 10px`.
  - Nav CTA: ghost style (`border:1px solid var(--color-border); background: transparent`).
  - Results: remove the alert. The plan title is the only text above 20 px in the top 600 px.
- **Priority:** High

### 2. Typography: **4/10**
- **Observation:**
  - Satoshi fails to load (CSP), so every visitor sees a system font.
  - Scale in use: 12 / 13 / 15 / 16 / 18 / 22 / 24–32 / 30–44 / 48 px. That's 9 sizes with 13 and 15 nearly indistinguishable.
  - Labels and helper text are 13 px (`.card .sub`, `.step-label`, form labels at 600 weight). Body is 15 px on desktop.
  - The app logo renders "Money Moves AU" with word gaps; the landing logo renders "MoneyMoves AU".
  - Debt names render lowercase ("credit card") in the table but title case in the select.
- **Proposed scale (7 steps):** 12 (legal) / 14 (labels, helper) / 16 (body, inputs) / 18 (card titles) / 22 (section titles) / 30 (page H1, mobile 26) / 44 (hero H1, mobile 32).
  - Line height 1.5 for body, 1.15 for headings.
  - Numbers: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` in the debt table and KPIs.
- **Fix:** Load the font (C4). Bump labels and helper text to 14 px. Fix the logo markup to `Money<span>Moves</span> AU` with no whitespace. Title-case debt names.
- **Priority:** High

### 3. Color and contrast: **6/10**

## Phase 8: Modern SaaS expectations

**Recommended (each tied to a demonstrated problem):**
- **Smart defaults** for HECS and typical rates (Phase 4).
- **Autosave of the full state.** Debts and step are currently lost on refresh (Phase 5, PayPal error).

**Deliberately not recommended:**
- A command palette, keyboard shortcuts or bulk actions: one-session consumer tool, no repeated tasks.

- Dashboards or activity history: there's no returning-user state, and building it would further support the "monthly" claim, which should go.
- Optimistic UI: there's nothing to optimistically commit.
